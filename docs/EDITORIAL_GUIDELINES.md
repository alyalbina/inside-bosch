# Editorial guidelines

The exact public-domain reproduction is visual evidence. Write observations from inspected crops. Do not infer species, identities, emotions or precise materials beyond what the image and cited source support. Rectangle bounds are visually checked, not automatically curator-certified.

## Claim types

| Type | Meaning | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Observation | What appears in the image | Exact reproduction reference and checked crop |
| Museum interpretation | A named museum’s reading | Source on the particular passage; attribution in text |
| Scholarly argument | A named author’s reading | Author and source; avoid treating it as consensus |
| Visual comparison | This project’s comparison of forms | Image reference; explicit distinction from proven shared meaning |

Every content entry has image evidence. Every interpretative assertion has claim-level source IDs. No fabricated quotations; no hidden-message certainty; no single final explanation. Observation-only entries must remain labelled as such. Dates follow Prado’s circa 1490–1500 range, not an invented exact date.

## Verification state

Initial entries were checked against the supplied reproduction and accessible museum/search-indexed excerpts on 12 October 2026. Some museum pages returned 403 when opened in this environment; cached excerpts were used only for the particular assertions they directly supported. This is source checking by the project author, not independent curator approval. The bibliography identifies material for readers to consult; a whole article was not silently reproduced.

Before marketing as a scholarly edition, an art historian should review each observation, claim, translation, bounding box and supporting passage. Record their actual name, date and approval only after review. Do not expand content by guessing a symbol’s conventional meaning.
